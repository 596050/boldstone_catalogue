# A cycle that never quite repeats itself

Moonspiral compresses the Moon's whole changing character into a single square panel, 55cm by 55cm, carved and cast in white marble stone: one continuous spiralling line standing in for the phases of the Moon, rather than a sequence of separate panels marking each stage individually. The choice of a spiral rather than a plain circle is a considered one. A circle suggests a cycle that returns to exactly the same point each time; a spiral suggests a cycle that repeats, broadly, but never quite arrives back where it started.

That distinction happens to be true of the real Moon. Its orbit around Earth is not a perfect circle but a slight ellipse, closer to Earth at one point, called perigee, and further away at another, called apogee. Under Kepler's second law of planetary motion, a body in an elliptical orbit sweeps out equal areas in equal times, which in practice means it moves faster when closer to Earth and slower when further away. The result is that the Moon's monthly journey through its phases does not proceed at a perfectly constant rate: near perigee it moves through its orbit more quickly, and near apogee more slowly, so the timing between one phase and the next shifts subtly from month to month rather than repeating on an absolutely fixed schedule. Combine that with the fact that a full cycle of phases, the synodic month, does not divide evenly into a calendar year, and the result is a rhythm that is dependable in its broad shape, full moon following new moon roughly every twenty-nine and a half days, without ever quite closing into an identical, repeating loop. A spiral is a genuinely appropriate shape for that kind of cycle: recognisable, repeating, and never exactly the same twist twice.

Moonspiral is carved by hand and then cast in white marble stone, its single sweeping line kept continuous across the full 55cm panel rather than broken into discrete stages. At 7cm deep, the carving holds a strong, consistent shadow along the length of the spiral, which shifts and rotates slightly in character as the light moves through the day, echoing, in a small way, the real Moon's own slightly uneven progress through its monthly cycle.

At a compact 55cm square, Moonspiral works well as a single, contained piece, suited to a smaller wall, a hallway, or a considered spot within a larger arrangement of other reliefs.
