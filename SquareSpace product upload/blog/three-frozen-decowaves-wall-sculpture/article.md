# Three waves, travelling together

Where a two-panel version of this design reads as a single call and response, Three Frozen Decowaves extends the same hard-edged, 1930s wave motif into a longer sequence: three panels, each 90cm by 15cm, carved in white marble stone, running to a full 45cm in height once hung with even gaps between them. The extra panel changes the piece's character more than its size alone suggests — three repetitions read as a continuing rhythm in a way that two cannot quite manage, closer to a sequence caught mid-motion than a single symmetrical pair.

Real ocean waves rarely travel alone. A single storm out at sea generates a whole set of waves of similar size and spacing, radiating outward together — what oceanographers call a wave train, a term with a fitting double meaning here, since a train of waves and a train of railway carriages both move as a linked, evenly spaced sequence. Surfers and coastal forecasters watch for wave trains specifically, because waves generated by the same weather system tend to arrive in sets, with a lull between one train and the next. Three Frozen Decowaves captures something of that rhythm, not as a literal transcription of the sea but through the stylised, hard-edged vocabulary of Art Deco design, in which a natural wave form was routinely simplified into a repeatable, geometric unit — exactly the kind of motif that decorated the stair rails and door grilles of the great streamlined ocean liners of the 1930s.

Each of the three panels is carved by hand and then cast in white marble stone, held to an 8cm depth that keeps every edge of the wave crisp rather than allowing it to soften into a naturalistic curve. With three panels rather than two, consistency between them matters even more: the wave form has to repeat with total confidence, each panel picking up exactly where the rhythm of the last one left off, so the eye reads one continuous train of motion rather than three separate objects. Under a raking light, the three waves throw a long, regular sequence of shadows that shifts together through the day; under flatter light, the sequence reads more as a single graphic band.

At 90cm wide per panel and a full 45cm tall as a group, excluding gaps, Three Frozen Decowaves has enough presence to work as the main feature on a long wall — a kitchen, a dining room, or a run of hallway long enough to give three evenly spaced panels room to breathe. Because the rhythm depends on consistent spacing, it is worth measuring and marking the gaps before hanging, rather than placing each panel by eye.
