# A shape people were carving five thousand years ago

Timeless Spiral takes the spiral out of any single specific context, natural or decorative, and presents it as what the sculpture's own name claims: an archetypal form, one of the oldest and most persistent shapes in human visual culture, carved here as a single panel, 45cm by 40cm, in white marble stone.

The spiral is a strong candidate for the oldest continuously used decorative motif in human history. At Newgrange, a passage tomb in Ireland built around three thousand two hundred years before the common era, older than Stonehenge and the pyramids of Giza, Neolithic builders carved elaborate spiral patterns directly into the massive kerbstones surrounding the monument, including a well-known triple spiral, three interlocking spiral arms, cut into the stone at the tomb's entrance. Nobody now knows with certainty what the spiral meant to the people who carved it there, whether it tracked the sun, the turning year, a journey into and out of the earth, or something else entirely, but its presence at a five-thousand-year-old monument confirms the shape was already considered worth carving in permanent stone long before recorded history began. The motif did not stop there: spiral ornament runs through Celtic art for millennia after Newgrange, appears independently in Aegean and Minoan pottery decoration, and turns up again and again across unrelated cultures worldwide, a shape simple enough to carve with the most basic tools and complex enough to reward long looking, which may be as good an explanation as any for why it has never gone out of use.

Part of the spiral's staying power may also be that it was never purely invented. It is a shape nature already supplies in abundance, from the shell of an ammonite to the horn of a ram, so early carvers reaching for a satisfying, recurring form had a great deal of natural precedent to draw on, whether or not they thought of it in those terms.

Timeless Spiral is carved by hand and then cast in white marble stone, kept as a single, confident, unbroken curve rather than a decorated or embellished one, closer in spirit to the plain, direct carving of an ancient stone monument than to a more ornamental modern pattern. At 5cm deep, the relief is one of the shallower pieces in the collection, relying on the clarity and confidence of the curve itself rather than dramatic depth, so it depends on a reasonably direct light to read well, the shadow along the spiral sharpening as the light lowers toward morning or evening.

At 45cm by 40cm, Timeless Spiral is a modestly sized, self-contained piece, well suited to a hallway, a study, or as one part of a considered grouping alongside other natural-form reliefs, its plain confidence making it easy to place without dominating a room.
