# The curve every palm frond eventually makes

Deco Spiral with Two Palms takes its central image from one of the more reliable shapes in the plant world: the long, arcing curve of a mature palm frond, bowing under its own weight the longer it grows. Three panels, each 45cm by 40cm, carved and cast in white marble stone, run to a full 120cm across, the Art Deco style flattening that organic curve into the confident, repeating line the movement favoured.

A palm tree grows from a single point. Unlike a broad-crowned oak or beech, most palms have just one growing tip, at the very top of the trunk, from which every new frond emerges in turn, tightly furled at first and then opening out over days or weeks. Those fronds are arranged around the crown in a spiral, each one emerging at a consistent angle of rotation from the last — the same underlying logic of efficient packing around a central stem that governs the arrangement of scales on a pinecone or seeds in a sunflower head, applied here at the scale of an entire tree crown. As each frond ages, it does not stay rigidly upright. Its own weight, unsupported by any woody branch, gradually pulls it into a longer and lower arc, so a mature palm crown shows fronds at every stage of that curve at once: young ones standing nearly vertical near the centre, older ones bowing further out and down, the oldest drooping almost to the horizontal before they eventually drop away. The elegant, sweeping curve Art Deco designers borrowed from the palm frond is not a stylised invention. It is closely observed from exactly how a real frond ages and falls.

The three panels are carved individually and then cast in white marble stone, each one holding a related but slightly different stage of the same curving line, so the sequence across the wall echoes the layered, staggered fronds of an actual palm crown rather than repeating one identical shape three times. Because the curves are broad and sweeping rather than tightly detailed, the piece depends on generous, confident shadow to read well: raking light traces the full length of each curve in one continuous line, while flatter light reduces the piece to a softer, more abstract pattern of light and dark, the individual fronds less distinct from one another.

At 45cm by 120cm across the three panels, Deco Spiral with Two Palms is a wide, striking piece suited to a large interior or garden wall — a dining room, a hallway with some length to it, or a garden wall visible from a distance, where its full sweep can be read as one gesture rather than viewed too close up. The three panels should be hung level and evenly spaced so the staggered curve reads as continuous.
