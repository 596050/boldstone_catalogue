# When a shoal moves as a single animal

Spiralfish Wave and Tide takes its subject from one of the more mesmerising sights in the sea: a shoal of fish, sometimes numbering in the thousands, wheeling and turning as a single coordinated mass, folding itself into spiralling, ribboning shapes with no visible leader and no apparent plan. Two panels, each 55cm square, carved and cast in white marble stone, together span 110cm, the spiral form of the shoal carried across both.

A shoal of fish achieves that seamless, spiralling coordination without any central direction at all. Each fish is simply responding to its handful of nearest neighbours, adjusting its own speed, direction and spacing moment to moment, and from those purely local, individual reactions, a much larger coordinated pattern emerges across the whole group, capable of turning, contracting and stretching into a spiral almost instantaneously. Biologists think shoaling behaviour like this persists because it offers real protection: a large, densely packed group can confuse a predator's ability to target any single fish, a phenomenon known as the confusion effect, while the sheer number of eyes scanning the water gives the group collective early warning of danger long before any one fish would notice alone. There is also growing evidence that swimming within a shoal may reduce the energy an individual fish needs to spend, in a manner not unlike cyclists drafting behind one another, each fish potentially gaining some hydrodynamic advantage from the vortices shed by the fish just ahead of it. None of this requires intelligence in any individual fish beyond a simple set of reflexes. The spiral the whole shoal traces through the water is a genuinely emergent pattern, built from thousands of small, local decisions rather than any single directing mind.

The two panels are carved by hand and then cast in white marble stone, the spiralling mass of the shoal worked in relief so individual fish forms blur into the larger curling movement rather than standing out as separate, distinct creatures, much as a real shoal reads from any distance as one fluid shape rather than as its individual members. At 7cm deep, the carving holds enough shadow to give the spiral real turning motion, the shadow deepening toward the centre of the curl and softening toward the outer edge, exactly where a real shoal would thin out at its margins.

At 55cm by 110cm across the pair, Spiralfish Wave and Tide is a substantial horizontal piece, suited to a dining room, a hallway with some real length, or a garden wall where its full spiralling sweep can be taken in from a slight distance. The two panels should be hung level and with a considered gap so the spiral reads as one continuous turning shape rather than two separate carved reliefs.
