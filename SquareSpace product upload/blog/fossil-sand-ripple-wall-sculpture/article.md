# The tide's last pattern, kept in stone for good

Fossil Sand Ripple is the companion piece to Smooth Sand Ripple, and the two share a subject: the same wind- and water-carved ripple pattern that appears wherever loose sand meets a moving current. Where its companion reads as the pattern still in motion, Fossil Sand Ripple takes its name, and its coarser, more heavily textured surface, from what happens when that pattern stops moving for good: when a rippled sand surface gets buried, compacted, and turned to stone. Carved and cast by hand in white marble stone, it measures the same 50cm by 100cm as its sibling piece.

Ripple marks are usually erased quickly. The next tide, or the next gust of wind, rearranges the sand within hours. Occasionally, though, a rippled surface gets buried under a fresh layer of sediment before it can be disturbed again, and if that burial is followed, over a very long span of time, by enough pressure and mineral cementation to turn the loose sand into solid rock, the ripple pattern itself survives, preserved as a fossil structure on the surface of a bed of sandstone. Geologists take these preserved ripple marks seriously as evidence: because water ripples and wind ripples have slightly different, recognisable profiles, and because ripples are subtly asymmetric in the direction the current or wind was moving, a fossil ripple mark can tell a geologist not only that the rock was once a beach, dune or riverbed, but which way the ancient current flowed, and even, in rock that has since been folded or overturned, which side used to face upward. It is a strikingly direct piece of evidence: stand on an exposed bedding plane of ancient sandstone today, and the ripples underfoot can be identical in form to the ones a retreating tide left an hour ago on a beach nearby, except that these have not moved in millions of years.

Fossil Sand Ripple is carved with a noticeably coarser, sand-like texture than its companion piece, closer to the gritty surface of an exposed ancient bedding plane than to a smooth, wind-polished dune face. That texture changes how the piece behaves in light: rather than one clean shadow line per ridge, the grain of the surface scatters the light across each ripple, giving the whole relief a slightly softer, more granular quality, especially noticeable as the day's light shifts from the low, warm angle of morning to the flatter light of midday and back again toward evening.

Fossil Sand Ripple works equally well paired with Smooth Sand Ripple along a long wall or hung entirely on its own, indoors or on an exterior patio wall, where its texture suits being picked out by artificial light after dark — a wall-mounted spotlight raking across the surface brings out the grain in a way that flat daylight sometimes cannot.
