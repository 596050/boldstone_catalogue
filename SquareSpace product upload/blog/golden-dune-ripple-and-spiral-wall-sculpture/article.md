# The metal that does not change

Golden Dune Ripple and Spiral takes the same dune-and-ripple vocabulary that runs through several pieces in this collection and finishes it in gold leaf rather than stone or patinated metal — a small piece, 35cm by 20cm, with the distinct, glimmering quality of a material that behaves quite differently from anything else in the collection once light hits it.

Gold owes its long history as a decorative material to two related properties. It is the most malleable of all metals, meaning it can be hammered into astonishingly thin sheets, historically by a goldbeater working the metal by hand, without cracking or tearing the way most other metals would; gold leaf can be beaten down to a few ten-thousandths of a millimetre, thin enough to let light glow through at its very edges. And, unlike copper, which reacts with the air over years to form the blue-green patina called verdigris, gold is chemically almost entirely inert: it does not oxidise, tarnish or corrode under ordinary conditions, which is why gilded objects recovered from tombs and treasuries thousands of years old can still gleam as brightly as the day they were made. Gilding, the technique of applying this beaten gold leaf to a surface, has been used for millennia on everything from ancient funerary objects to mediaeval manuscript illumination to picture frames and religious statuary, prized precisely because it does not need to be renewed or protected the way a painted or patinated surface does.

Golden Dune Ripple and Spiral is carved first, in the same way as the collection's stone pieces, before being finished in gold leaf rather than left as bare white marble stone. Because gold leaf is reflective rather than matte, the piece behaves quite differently in changing light compared with the stone reliefs elsewhere in the collection: instead of a soft, graduated shadow moving slowly across a textured surface, the gold catches and throws back a much sharper, more mobile glint of light, flaring and dimming as the angle of a candle flame or a lamp shifts, rather than shifting gradually the way daylight moves across matte stone through the day. It is a piece built for close, changeable, indoor light rather than the steady arc of the sun.

At a compact 35cm by 20cm, Golden Dune Ripple and Spiral suits a smaller, more intimate setting — a dining room wall where candlelight is likely, a bedroom, or a spot at the end of a corridor where a single lamp can catch it directly. Because gold leaf performs best under exactly this kind of close, warm, indoor light, it is worth thinking about where evening lighting will actually fall before choosing its position.
