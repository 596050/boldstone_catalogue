# A Colour Beetles Rarely Wear

Beetles make up the single largest group of animals on Earth by number of described species, more than a quarter of all known animal species between them, and the overwhelming majority of that diversity is expressed in black, brown or a metallic green or blue rather than pink. This print, one of a series of watercolours called Crazy Creatures, takes a beetle rendered in pink specifically because that colour sits outside what the group usually offers, and the choice is worth pausing on rather than passing over.

A beetle's colour is carried in its elytra, the hardened, modified pair of forewings that meet in a straight line down the back and protect the delicate flight wings folded beneath them. Elytra get their colour in one of two quite different ways. Pigment colour comes from chemical compounds laid down in the cuticle itself, melanins for black and brown, carotenoids obtained from the beetle's diet for some reds and yellows. Structural colour, responsible for the electric greens, blues and coppers seen in many jewel beetles and some weevils, is not a pigment at all but the result of extremely fine, regularly layered structures in the cuticle that reflect only certain wavelengths of light through interference, the same physical phenomenon behind the colours of a soap bubble or an oil film. True pink in a beetle is comparatively unusual regardless of which mechanism produces it, which is part of why a pink beetle reads, correctly, as a slightly "crazy" creature rather than a typical one, and why it makes a good subject for a series built around exactly that sense of the natural world's stranger edges.

Watercolour suits an exoskeleton like this well, because the elytra's hard, glossy surface can be suggested through controlled highlight and thin, layered washes far more convincingly than through flat, opaque colour, and a beetle's own considerable structural detail, its jointed legs, its long antennae, the fine sculpting on the surface of the elytra, rewards the kind of close observational drawing this series is built from.
