# Two ornaments older than Art Deco, fused into one

Deco Palm and Spiral takes two of the most persistent motifs in the whole history of architectural ornament and folds them into a single two-part relief: a stylised palm frond, and beside it, a spiral. Both panels measure 30cm square, carved and cast in white marble stone, spanning 60cm together — modest in size, but carrying two ideas each considerably older than the Art Deco style credited with reviving them here.

The palm frond has been a decorative subject for as long as people have been carving columns. Ancient Egyptian temple architecture used palm-frond capitals — the tops of stone columns carved to fan out like the crown of a date palm — as one of its standard forms, alongside papyrus-bundle capitals, both drawn straight from the plants that grew along the Nile. The spiral scroll, meanwhile, has its own separate deep history: it is the defining feature of the Ionic order in ancient Greek architecture, where paired volutes curl outward from the top of a column like a rolled scroll of paper. Both motifs survived, in one adapted form or another, through classical revival after classical revival, and both were picked up again by Art Deco designers in the early twentieth century, who valued them for the same reason the ancients did: they are forms that read clearly at a glance, symmetrical enough to repeat along a frieze or a doorway, and flexible enough to simplify further into pure geometry. Deco Palm and Spiral puts the two side by side, much as historical capitals sometimes did, but strips both back to the plainest possible version of the idea.

Carved first, then cast, the two panels are kept in white marble stone with a firm, confident edge to each curve — the palm frond rendered as a fan of clean lines rather than the more organic, overlapping fronds of a real tree, and the spiral cut as a single unbroken groove rather than a softened swirl. That crispness is what lets the piece hold a sharp line of shadow: a low, raking light draws a hard edge along every rib of the palm and around the full turn of the spiral, while flatter, more direct light lets the same forms recede into a gentler, more legible pattern. Because the piece is only 6cm deep, it depends more on the confidence of its line than on any dramatic depth of undercut.

At 30cm x 60cm across the pair, Deco Palm and Spiral is a modestly scaled piece, suited to a hallway, a small dining room wall, or a spot alongside other similarly sized reliefs rather than needing to command a large room alone. Its two panels should be hung with a consistent gap so the palm and the spiral read as a considered pairing rather than two unrelated fragments.
